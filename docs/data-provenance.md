# Data provenance

Every numeric dataset in this tree gets a row here: element masses, fit
coefficients, optical data, experimental points. A dataset without a row is a
CI-review blocker.

The allowed origins are:

1. **Computed from a published formula.** Cite the paper; the code is ours.
2. **Experimental measurement.** Cite the paper or database record.
   Measurements are facts; we cite them.
3. **A permissive source** (Tier A in [`../CONTRIBUTING.md`](../CONTRIBUTING.md)),
   with its notice kept in `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.md`.

The following are **never allowed**:

- SRIM tables, including any that other projects pass along.
- ICRU report tables.
- NIST SRD tables, until their terms have been reviewed and recorded here.
- Anything whose origin is not known.

## Optical data for dielectric-function models: inventory

`electron::data::OpticalElf` holds a tabulated optical energy-loss function
`Im[-1/ε(ω)]` and refuses data without a provenance. The operator ruled on
2026-10-06 (#34) that measured optical constants may be committed as **cited
facts**, as the measured sputter yields are. Committed so far (#98): Al and Cu,
from Hagemann, Gudat and Kunz (1975), in `validation/data/optical/` (rows in
the table above). **Still not committed:** Si (no source found that covers the
valence region and can be opened, below), SiO₂, PMMA and W. The Nebula/cstool
`.dat` files stay unusable regardless of the ruling (unknown origin).

The table records candidates, not choices. "Confirmed" means the terms were
read on the date given; everything else is an open question. A permissive
licence on an aggregator or on code that ships data does not establish the
terms of the measurements it carries. Citations were checked against
Crossref on 2026-10-06; the papers themselves were not read in this pass.

## Open questions

- IAEA stopping database reuse terms: unread (bot wall on 2026-10-04).
  Resolve before ingesting points.
- NIST ESTAR/PSTAR terms under 15 USC 290e: unresolved.
- Optical data for dielectric-function models: the #34 ruling (2026-10-06)
  allows measured optical constants as cited facts. Al and Cu are committed
  (#98), checked against the scanned tables of DESY report SR-74/7. Both miss
  the 5 % f-sum check with the linear interpolation `OpticalElf::elf()`
  serves (Al N_eff +13.8 % and P_eff +9.4 %; Cu N_eff +7.3 %), and both are
  pinned in the test. With power-law segments Cu passes (-4.1 %) and Al's
  N_eff is +7.5 % (the authors' own Table 10: +4.5 %; the rest is quadrature
  on the printed knots). The excess is the representation on log-spaced
  knots, not a transcription error. A denser table (committing the report's
  printed ELF column, or a later Al source such as Shiles et al. (1980))
  would remove it; both are open. Si (no openable source across 6 to 30 eV;
  tracked in #125), SiO₂ (no valence-region source),
  PMMA-class resists and W remain to be sourced; candidates are in the
  inventory above.
- Electron band parameters (work function, Fermi energy, electron affinity,
  band gap, valence band width) for built-in material defaults: no open
  source citable to a table was found on 2026-10-07 (row above). Candidates
  are the Michaelson (1977) and Derry et al. (2015) work-function
  compilations, read from a copy the operator can supply, or a measured
  value per material from an open-access paper. Until then callers supply
  the values with their provenance.
- Penn, Phys. Rev. B 35, 482 (1987): the single-pole Penn model is
  implemented from the account in Shinotsuka et al., Surf. Interface Anal.
  49, 238 (2017) (row above), because the original is closed access. Its own
  equation numbers are therefore not cited, and the dispersion relation was
  not compared against the original. A reader with access should confirm
  that eq. (8) of the 2017 paper is Penn's dispersion as published.
- Fröhlich LO-phonon and polaron-trapping channels (#102): the primary
  papers (Fröhlich 1954, Llacer & Garwin 1969, Ganachaud & Mokrani 1995)
  were not reachable; the equations come from two open-access restatements
  (rows above). Polaron constants `C`, `γ` are a gap for every material.
- EADL / EPICS2017 subshell binding energies: resolved. The operator ruled
  on 2026-10-06 (https://github.com/2AMLogic/lindhard/issues/34#issuecomment-6030285546) that the numbers may be committed as cited
  facts with the credit IAEA-NDS-224 Rev. 1 requests; see the row above.

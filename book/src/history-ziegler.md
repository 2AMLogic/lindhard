# James F. Ziegler (1935–2024)

James F. Ziegler died on 14 May 2024, aged 89 [O24]. A great deal of what the
field of ion stopping shares today, its compiled measurements, its
bibliography, its reference books and its best-known simulation program,
exists because of his work. lindhard builds on that legacy, as an
independent implementation written from the published physics. This page
records his professional life and work. It is not written or endorsed by
Ziegler or his estate, and nothing here implies that they endorse lindhard.

## Life and career

Ziegler was born on 20 April 1935 in Gary, Indiana [O24]. He studied at Yale
University, where he completed a Ph.D. in physics in 1967 [B-JFZ]. The
obituary adds that he began in electrical engineering, served in the US Navy,
and studied physics at the University of Cincinnati before returning to
Yale [O24].

He joined IBM Research in 1967 and stayed until 2000, mostly at Yorktown
Heights, New York. He worked on ion channeling, ion implantation for
making semiconductor devices, and radiation damage, and from 1975 to 2000
managed IBM's radiation science group [B-JFZ]. He then taught as a professor
at the US Naval Academy in Annapolis, Maryland [B-JFZ, O24].

He was elected a Fellow of the American Physical Society (1980) and a
Fellow of the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers (1996), and
received a von Humboldt Senior Scientist prize (1987) for work on the
theory of ion implantation [B-JFZ, O24].

## Work

**The *Stopping and Ranges of Ions in Matter* series.** Between 1977 and
1985 Ziegler wrote or co-wrote a run of volumes that collected stopping
powers and range distributions for ions in all elements, with Andersen
(hydrogen), himself (helium), and Littmark (range distributions) [AZ77,
Z78, LZ80, Z80]. The first volume of the series, with Biersack and
Littmark, is *The Stopping and Range of Ions in Solids* (Pergamon, 1985)
[ZBL85]. It contains the universal interatomic potential known as ZBL
and the computational methods behind SRIM; lindhard's
[ZBL potential](models/potentials.md) is taken from the published
description of it.

**SRIM.** With Biersack and other co-authors he developed the program, first
TRIM [BH80] and then SRIM, that made Monte Carlo
calculation of ion ranges routine in laboratories and fabs [ZZB10]. SRIM has been distributed since the mid-1980s. The
SRIM site, srim.org, also carries his historical review of the field [S03];
see the [history chapter](history.md).

**Compiled measurements.** He assembled, over decades, a bibliography of
published measurements of stopping and range, and used the measurements it indexes in his stopping-power volumes [Z77].
Preserving that literature is part of why this chapter exists.

**Cosmic rays and electronics.** In 1979 Ziegler and Lanford showed that
cosmic-ray secondaries can flip bits in computer memories at sea level
[ZL79]. Ziegler returned to the subject for years afterwards [Z96], and the
1979 paper became a starting point for the study of soft errors in
terrestrial electronics [B-JFZ].
